# What it does

For the topology diagram and the full documentation index see the [README](../../README.md).

`restream-controller` sits between OBS and your streaming platforms on a VPS you control. OBS publishes to the VPS — over RTMP, or over SRT carrying several audio tracks at once — and the VPS relays each platform its own feed from it. As long as the connection between the VPS and a platform stays open, that platform keeps the channel live — even while your own connection to the VPS is down.

- **Sources and platforms, decoupled.** A **source** is one OBS publish on the VPS — plain RTMP, RTMP with OBS's own Twitch VOD Track already embedded, or SRT carrying up to 6 audio tracks. A **platform** is one output — Twitch, YouTube, Kick, whatever — and picks exactly one source, plus which of its audio track(s) to send. Several platforms can share the same source with different picks — e.g. Twitch gets the mix with music, YouTube gets a clean track — from a single OBS publish, no second output needed.
- **RTMP+VOD platforms (Twitch VOD Track).** A platform can build its own live-mix + VOD-only-mix pair from any two (or the same) track of its source and push both to the platform in one embedded stream — reproducing Twitch's "VOD Track" feature even when the source itself doesn't carry one.
- **SRT platforms can carry multiple tracks too.** An SRT platform maps up to 6 independent output audio slots to any of its source's tracks (duplicates and remapping allowed, changed live without a reconnect). Most receivers still only accept a single audio track over SRT — use multitrack output only against a platform confirmed to accept it.
- **Live-toggleable, grouped platforms.** Every platform can be turned on and off **live** from the dashboard's Control tab, without restarting anything and without interrupting the platforms already streaming. Platforms are organized into groups with a shared group toggle — flip a whole group at once, or individual platforms within it.
- **Seamless fallback.** If a source disconnects unexpectedly (bad internet, crashed PC, closed OBS), every platform fed by it switches to a backup video instead of ending. Viewers see a placeholder, not a "stream offline" screen.
- **Seamless recovery.** When the source reconnects, the service waits for a clean keyframe from the live feed and cuts back to it without ever dropping the platform connections — no visible freeze, no re-buffering.
- **Fallback presets, not just a looping file.** A backup can be a single looping clip, or a **sequence** (an intro clip, a loop, and an outro played once before cutting back to live) or a **folder** of clips shuffled at random with an optional separator between them. Each platform picks a preset; the same preset can be reused across several platforms. The material lives in `media/` on the server and is managed from the dashboard — upload by drag & drop, browse folders, rename and delete — so adding a clip doesn't mean an SSH session.
- **A slow/broken platform never affects the others.** Each platform has its own output pipeline; if one can't keep up or its key is wrong, it retries (or stops) on its own while the rest keep streaming.
- **Contract-checked sources.** Each source declares what it should carry (type, number of audio tracks); the service verifies the real stream against that declaration as soon as it connects and holds its platforms back on a mismatch, instead of starting a broadcast with the wrong tracks.
- **Aggregate failsafe.** A broadcast is stopped hard (and OBS is told to stop) only if **none** of its enabled platforms can be reached at the start — a wrong key on one platform, while others connect, just drops that one.
- **Graceful stop, not just a timeout.** Consciously clicking "Stop Streaming" in OBS ends the broadcast immediately and cleanly, with no backup video and no waiting around — detected automatically by an invisible OBS Browser Source, no button to click.
- **Automatic timeout.** If a source doesn't come back within a configurable window (30 minutes by default), its platforms end the broadcast on their own instead of looping the backup video forever.
- **No Docker, no heavy dependencies.** The controller is one stdlib-only Go binary with the dashboard embedded in it — including its own RTMP push client for RTMP+VOD and Enhanced Broadcasting platforms. Around it: [MediaMTX](https://github.com/bluenviron/mediamtx) for RTMP/SRT ingest, `ffmpeg` for relaying, `srt-tools` for SRT read-back and multitrack SRT output. Nothing else is fetched at build time — `go.mod` has no requirements.

Everything is relayed with `-c copy` (no re-encoding on the VPS): every platform gets exactly the bitrate/codec OBS produces. This is a focused tool, not a general platform — no transcoding ladder, no per-platform resolution. One or more OBS publishes fanned out to as many destinations as you enable, each with its own audio pick, kept alive through drops.
